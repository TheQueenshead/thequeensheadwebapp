The oldest timbers in the Queen's Head were felled around 1340, which makes the building older than the Black Death, the Peasants' Revolt and the printing press.

## A hall house

It began life as a hall house — a single tall room open to the rafters, with a fire in the middle of the floor and no chimney. Soot from those fires still blackens some of the roof timbers upstairs. The chimney stack and first floor were added in the Tudor period, when it seems to have become an inn for the first time.

## The coaching years

By the 1700s Littlebury sat on the main road from London to Cambridge and Newmarket, and the Queen's Head was a coaching inn with stabling for twenty horses. The arch you walk through to reach the car park is the original coach entrance, and the Coach House room really was a coach house.

## Court and community

Records from the 1800s show the pub doubling as a manorial court and, on one memorable occasion, an inquest. During the Second World War it billeted airmen from the nearby airfield at Debden.

## Today

We took the inn on in the belief that a village pub should be the living room of its village. We hope you'll agree it still is.
